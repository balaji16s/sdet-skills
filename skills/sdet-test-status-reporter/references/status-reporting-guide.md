# Test Status Reporting Guide

Choose content based on the audience and decision. A daily team update should be short; a release or completion report may need stronger evidence and traceability.

## Useful Information Areas

### Progress

- planned versus completed analysis, design, implementation, environment, data, and execution work;
- tests passed, failed, blocked, not run, not applicable, or inconclusive;
- effort, schedule, and forecast where reliable.

### Product Quality

- behavior demonstrated and important failures observed;
- open and resolved defects by relevant impact or priority;
- quality measurements such as response time, availability, accessibility, or reliability when in scope;
- confirmation and regression outcomes.

### Risk and Coverage

- mitigation status of important product risks;
- requirements, rules, states, code, platforms, data, workflows, or other defined coverage;
- important untested, weakly tested, or blocked areas;
- remaining risk and confidence limits.

### Delivery Health

- environment, build, data, tool, dependency, staffing, or scope problems;
- changes since the previous report;
- control actions, owners, target dates, and decisions needed.

## Suggested Status Report

### 1. Decision Summary

In a few sentences, state overall progress, the strongest quality evidence, the main concern, forecast, and decision or help needed.

### 2. Scope and Data

State the release or build, test scope, reporting period, cutoff time, data sources, and important metric definitions.

### 3. Objectives and Progress

| Objective or work area | Planned | Actual | Status | Evidence or explanation |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Use the team's status labels and define them when unclear.

### 4. Quality, Risk, and Coverage

Explain significant results, defect impact, covered and uncovered risks, relevant coverage, and remaining uncertainty. Avoid long lists that hide the main message.

### 5. Blockers, Forecast, and Actions

| Issue or action | Effect | Owner or decision role | Target | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### 6. Data Limitations

State missing results, stale links, unstable environments, small samples, changed scope, inconsistent definitions, or other reasons for reduced confidence.

## Completion Additions

For a test completion report, also include:

- achieved and unmet objectives or exit criteria;
- planned versus delivered testware and execution;
- unresolved defects and remaining risks;
- known limitations and accepted deviations;
- stored evidence, configuration, and handover details;
- lessons and improvement actions.

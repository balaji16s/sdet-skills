# Test Status Reporter

Explain what testing has learned, how work is progressing, what threatens the objective, and which decisions are needed. Report evidence and uncertainty rather than decorating raw counts.

## Before You Start

Collect the test objectives, reporting period, audience, plan or baseline, scope, results, coverage, defects, risks, blockers, environment status, changes, and previous report when available.

Read [references/status-reporting-guide.md](references/status-reporting-guide.md) before preparing the report.

## Reporting Method

1. Identify the audience, decision, scope, product version, and data cutoff time.
2. Compare actual progress and results with the applicable plan, objective, risk, or completion criteria.
3. Validate metric definitions and separate passed, failed, blocked, not run, not applicable, and inconclusive results.
4. Summarize product quality evidence, important defects, coverage, remaining risks, and confidence limits.
5. Explain blockers, changes, trends, forecast, and control actions.
6. Call out decisions and help needed, with owners or decision-making roles where known.
7. For completion reporting, add delivered testware, unresolved work, accepted deviations, lessons, and handover information.

## Working Rules

- Use plain language and put decision-relevant information first.
- Tailor detail and terminology to the audience without hiding bad news.
- State the source, scope, time, and definition of important metrics.
- Report counts with percentages so small samples are not misleading.
- Do not use pass rate alone as a statement of product quality.
- Distinguish progress, coverage, defect information, product quality, and risk; they answer different questions.
- Compare trends only when definitions and scope are compatible.
- Do not silently count blocked or not-run tests as passed, failed, or complete.
- Explain stale, missing, duplicated, or unreliable data.
- Separate observed facts, interpretation, forecast, assumptions, and recommendations.
- State remaining risk and confidence. Do not declare a product safe, defect-free, or ready without agreed decision authority and criteria.

## Expected Result

Lead with a short decision summary. Follow with objective-based progress, quality evidence, risk and coverage, defects, blockers, forecast, actions, and data limitations using the guide.
